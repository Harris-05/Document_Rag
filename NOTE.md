# Note

**Quote verification.** The model proposes quotes; my code decides whether they are real. Each is searched for in the document's stored text after folding whitespace, case, ligatures, quote and dash styles, line-break hyphenation and end punctuation. An ellipsis splits a quote into parts that must appear in order. Page numbers from the model are never trusted; they only choose between repeated matches. A match maps back to character offsets for highlighting, and each quote is checked against its own document. A quote that is not found is marked "not found" and cannot be opened. It can fail where extraction garbles tables or columns (a genuine quote is rejected) and on scanned pages, and it cannot judge meaning: a verified quote proves the words exist, not that they support the claim.

**Large documents.** Pages are split into chunks of about 1,100 characters that never cross a page and keep their section heading. Every chunk is indexed (keyword plus embeddings, rank-fused). A planner, a judge and up to three corrective rounds follow. Each answer reports the pages and sections searched, unreadable pages are flagged, a "not found" reply lists what was searched, and thin evidence is labelled partial, so it never claims to have read what it did not. Measured on 150 pages.

**Part C.** I chose Option 2 (agentic research). It is closest to my own work and reuses my loop, verifier and streaming; Option 1 is not attempted. The model calls `search_document`, `get_section` and `list_clauses` in a loop, with each step shown live. Caps (6 rounds, 4 calls per round, 4 invalid calls) are enforced in code, malformed or invented calls get an error back instead of crashing, and the answer is written from what was read, with its quotes verified. The hardest part was keeping a model-driven loop bounded and honest: models skip tools, send bad arguments or cite without quoting. Tested with a scripted model and three real slide decks, less on real contracts.
